Most people think of gout as an old-fashioned ailment — a suddenly excruciating big toe. It's real and painful, but the more useful way to think about gout and its underlying marker, uric acid, is as a **window into your metabolic health**. High uric acid rarely travels alone.

## What uric acid is

Uric acid is a waste product from breaking down purines (found in certain foods and made by the body). When levels get high enough — generally above **6.8 mg/dL**, the definition of hyperuricemia — it can crystallize in joints and trigger the inflammation of a gout attack ([Role of Diet in Hyperuricemia and Gout, NCBI](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7886025/)).

## The stat most people don't know

Here's the part that reframes gout entirely: **over 71% of gout patients aged 40 and older have metabolic syndrome** — compared with about 22% of US adults overall ([American Journal of Medicine](https://www.amjmed.com/article/S0002-9343(06)00890-4/fulltext)). High uric acid is now considered a **marker of insulin resistance**, because high insulin makes the kidneys hold onto uric acid. So an elevated uric acid isn't just about joints — it's often a flag for the same metabolic picture behind [insulin resistance](/blog/how-to-know-if-you-have-insulin-resistance/) and [prediabetes](/blog/reversing-prediabetes/).

## What your level means

- **Below 6.8 mg/dL** — within range for most adults
- **Above 6.8 mg/dL** — hyperuricemia; raises gout risk and flags metabolic risk
- **During an acute attack** — uric acid can paradoxically read normal, so timing matters

Not everyone with high uric acid develops gout, and gout is diagnosed clinically (sometimes by analyzing joint fluid) — not by the number alone. But the number still carries metabolic information worth acting on.

## The metabolic connection

Because uric acid clusters with high [triglycerides, low HDL](/blog/cholesterol-numbers-explained/), high blood pressure, and elevated fasting insulin, it's effectively part of the [metabolic syndrome](/blog/metabolic-syndrome-markers/) picture. That means the same levers — reducing sugary drinks and excess alcohol, improving insulin sensitivity, losing excess weight — tend to help uric acid and metabolic health together.

## What to do

1. **Know your uric acid number** — it's a standard, inexpensive test.
2. **Read it in metabolic context** — high uric acid often signals insulin resistance, so check glucose, insulin, and lipids too.
3. **Address the shared drivers** — alcohol, sugary/fructose intake, excess weight.
4. **See a clinician** for recurrent gout or significantly elevated levels — it may need specific treatment beyond diet.
