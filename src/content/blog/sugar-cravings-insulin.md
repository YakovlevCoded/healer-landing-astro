If you're reaching for something sweet every afternoon and blaming your lack of willpower, it's worth reconsidering. Frequent, intense sugar cravings are less about character and more about **biology** — specifically, what your blood sugar and insulin are doing across the day. And that biology is testable.

## The blood-sugar roller coaster

Here's the cycle behind most cravings: you eat something high in refined carbs, blood sugar spikes, and your body releases a burst of insulin to bring it back down. If that insulin response is exaggerated — as it often is in [early insulin resistance](/blog/how-to-know-if-you-have-insulin-resistance/) — glucose overshoots downward into a relative low. Your brain reads that dip as an emergency and demands fast energy: more sugar. You eat it, and the cycle repeats.

That's why cravings so often come with the classic **afternoon energy crash** — they're two symptoms of the same swing.

## The stat most people don't know

About **96 million US adults — more than 1 in 3 — have prediabetes, and over 80% don't know it** ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes/php/data-research/index.html)) — because standard testing checks glucose, which stays normal for years, rather than [fasting insulin](/blog/fasting-insulin-test-guide/), which rises early ([StatPearls / NCBI](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK507839/)). So the very mechanism driving relentless cravings is often present and undetected, dismissed as a sweet tooth.

## The markers to check

- **Fasting insulin** — the earliest signal that your body is overproducing insulin to manage blood sugar.
- **Fasting glucose** — pairs with insulin to calculate **HOMA-IR**, the practical insulin-resistance score.
- **HbA1c** — your 3-month blood-sugar average; useful, but a [lagging indicator](/blog/hba1c-explained/) that only rises later.

When cravings come with fatigue, midsection weight that won't budge, or a family history of diabetes, these markers usually tell the story.

## What to do

1. **Check insulin, not just glucose** — ask specifically for fasting insulin so HOMA-IR can be calculated.
2. **Notice the pattern** — cravings plus afternoon crashes plus midsection weight point toward the blood-sugar swing.
3. **Track over time** — one reading is a snapshot; a trend shows whether it's improving.
4. **Talk to a clinician** about diet and lifestyle steps that steady blood sugar — protein, fiber, and meal timing often help.
