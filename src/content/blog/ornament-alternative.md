If you've gone looking for an app to make sense of your blood test results, Ornament probably came up. It's one of the more established players in the lab-tracking space, and for good reason — it does the basics well. But "the basics" is also where the ceiling is, so it's worth knowing exactly what you get and what you don't before you commit your years of lab history to it.

## The stat most people don't know

Most adults have blood test results scattered across **multiple portals, PDFs, and photos** going back years — and can't actually retrieve or read them as a trend. That's the problem every app in this category is trying to solve. The differences come down to *how far past simple storage* each one goes.

## What Ornament does well

- **Easy upload** — photo and PDF recognition pulls values from your results without much typing.
- **Organized history** — markers grouped by body system, with a timeline for each.
- **Color-coded status** — a quick green/yellow/red read against reference ranges.
- **Clean, approachable design** — it's not intimidating for non-medical users.

For someone who mainly wants a tidy digital archive of their labs, it's a reasonable pick.

## Where it falls short

- **Reference range, not optimal range.** Like most trackers, it flags values against the wide "normal" range — so a functionally low ferritin or a borderline thyroid can still read as fine. (See [reference vs optimal ranges](/blog/how-to-read-blood-test-results/).)
- **Limited derived markers.** Scores that require combining values — like HOMA-IR for [insulin resistance](/blog/how-to-know-if-you-have-insulin-resistance/) — aren't its focus.
- **Storage over interpretation.** It's better at *holding* your data than at explaining what the pattern across markers actually means.
- **Symptoms live separately.** How you feel — fatigue, brain fog — isn't tied back to the numbers.

## What to look for in an alternative

1. **Derived metabolic markers** calculated automatically (HOMA-IR, triglyceride-to-HDL).
2. **Optimal-range flagging**, not just the wide normal band.
3. **Symptom-to-lab connections** — linking [fatigue](/blog/always-tired-blood-markers/) or [brain fog](/blog/brain-fog-blood-markers/) to the markers behind them.
4. **Trends read together**, so a normal [HbA1c](/blog/hba1c-explained/) sitting on a rising HOMA-IR doesn't slip through.
