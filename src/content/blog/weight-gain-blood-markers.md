Few things are more frustrating than gaining weight when you haven't changed what you eat or how much you move. It's easy to blame willpower — but when the scale climbs without an obvious behavioral reason, the driver is often **metabolic and hormonal**, sitting quietly in blood work nobody thought to check.

The reassuring part: the most common causes are testable, and once you know which one is at play, the approach becomes a lot clearer.

## The stat most people don't know

The CDC estimates about **96 million US adults — more than 1 in 3 — have prediabetes**, the stage that follows insulin resistance, and over 80% don't know it ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes/php/data-research/index.html)). One of its hallmark effects is weight that's hard to lose and easy to gain, because high insulin is a fat-storage signal. Most people don't know they have it, because standard screening measures glucose (which stays normal for years) rather than [fasting insulin](/blog/fasting-insulin-test-guide/), which rises early ([StatPearls / NCBI](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK507839/)). Add thyroid issues — undiagnosed hypothyroidism affects several percent of adults, most of it subclinical ([JCEM](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/109/2/e657/7279439)) — and a lot of "unexplained" weight becomes very explainable.

## The metabolic panel worth checking

- **Thyroid (TSH ± free T4)** — an underactive thyroid slows metabolism and is a classic cause of weight gain, alongside fatigue and feeling cold. ([High TSH, explained](/blog/high-tsh-thyroid-explained/).)
- **Fasting insulin + glucose (HOMA-IR)** — high insulin promotes fat storage and blocks fat burning. This is the big one, and it shows up before glucose does. ([How to know if you have insulin resistance](/blog/how-to-know-if-you-have-insulin-resistance/).)
- **HbA1c** — your 3-month blood-sugar average; a rising value tracks with worsening metabolic health. ([What HbA1c means](/blog/hba1c-explained/).)
- **Cortisol** — chronically elevated stress hormone drives appetite and central (belly) weight gain.

When two or more of these line up, the weight usually has a real metabolic driver rather than a behavioral one — and the same insulin resistance often drives [fatty liver (NAFLD)](/blog/fatty-liver-nafld-labs/), which travels in the same cluster.

## Why "normal" glucose isn't reassurance

Here's the trap: you can be gaining weight from insulin resistance while your fasting glucose and even HbA1c still read normal. That's because your body raises insulin to keep glucose in range — so the fat-storage signal (high insulin) is already firing while the marker most doctors check (glucose) still looks fine. Without a fasting insulin test, that driver is invisible.

## What to do

1. **Ask for the metabolic panel above** — specifically name fasting insulin and thyroid, since these get skipped.
2. **Rule out thyroid first** — it's a simple test and a common, very treatable cause.
3. **Check insulin, not just glucose** — the earliest and most common driver hides there.
4. **Track the trend** — metabolic change is gradual; a series of tests shows whether you're improving.
5. **Talk to a clinician** to build the right plan around what the numbers show.
