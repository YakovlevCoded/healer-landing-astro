Ferritin gets all the attention in iron testing, and for good reason — it's the best single marker of your iron stores. But "best single marker" isn't the same as "the whole story." A full iron panel adds three more numbers that, read together, distinguish genuine iron deficiency from other explanations and catch cases ferritin alone would miss.

## The stat most people don't know

Iron deficiency is the **most common nutritional deficiency in the world**, affecting up to a quarter of the global population ([WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/anaemia)). Yet it's frequently under-diagnosed, partly because a single "normal" ferritin can be reassuring while missing the fuller picture — especially when inflammation is in play.

## The four markers and what each adds

- **Ferritin — your iron stores.** The reservoir. Low ferritin is the earliest and most specific sign of depleted iron; below ~30 ng/mL is highly specific for deficiency ([AAFP](https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2013/0115/p98.html)). (See [what low ferritin means](/blog/low-ferritin-guide/) and the [ferritin levels chart](/blog/ferritin-levels-chart/).)
- **Serum iron — iron circulating right now.** A snapshot of iron currently in your blood. It fluctuates through the day and with recent meals, so it's weak on its own.
- **TIBC (total iron-binding capacity) — spare capacity.** Measures how much your transport protein (transferrin) *could* carry. TIBC tends to rise when iron is low (the body makes more transporters to grab what iron it can).
- **Transferrin saturation — the percentage filled.** Serum iron divided by capacity. Normal is roughly **20–50%**; below ~20% points to iron deficiency ([NIH Office of Dietary Supplements](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iron-HealthProfessional/)).

## Why the panel beats ferritin alone

The big reason is **inflammation**. Ferritin is an acute-phase reactant — it rises with infection, inflammation, or liver issues. So someone who's genuinely iron deficient *and* has some inflammation can show a ferritin that looks "normal," hiding the problem.

That's where transferrin saturation earns its place: a **low transferrin saturation alongside a normal-looking ferritin** can reveal iron deficiency that ferritin alone would have missed. The pattern across all four markers tells you what's really going on.

## Reading the patterns

- **Iron deficiency:** low ferritin, low serum iron, high TIBC, low transferrin saturation
- **Iron deficiency with inflammation:** ferritin may be normal/high, but transferrin saturation stays low
- **Iron overload:** high ferritin, high serum iron, high transferrin saturation

## What to do

1. **Ask for the full panel** — ferritin, serum iron, TIBC, and transferrin saturation — not just ferritin.
2. **Watch transferrin saturation** if you have inflammation, which can inflate ferritin.
3. **Track over time** — iron status shifts gradually; the trend is the story.
4. **Talk to a clinician** before supplementing — too much iron is also harmful.
