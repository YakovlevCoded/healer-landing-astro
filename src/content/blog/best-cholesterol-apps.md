"Your cholesterol is fine" is one of the most common — and most misleading — things you can hear at a checkup. Cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of death worldwide ([WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/cardiovascular-diseases-(cvds))), and cholesterol isn't one number — it's LDL, HDL, triglycerides, non-HDL, ApoB, and the ratios between them. Tracking the full picture over time needs the right app.

## Why one number isn't enough

Total cholesterol blends "good" and "bad" together, so it can hide a genuinely risky pattern — low HDL with high triglycerides — behind a reassuring-looking total. What matters is the [breakdown and the ratios](/blog/cholesterol-numbers-explained/), tracked over time. That's exactly what most apps don't do well.

### General health apps (Apple Health, etc.) — limited for lipids
Can store a total cholesterol or LDL value and show a basic timeline, but don't track the full panel, calculate ratios, or flag optimal ranges. (See [Apple Health vs a dedicated lab tracker](/blog/apple-health-vs-lab-tracker/).)

### Testing services (InsideTracker, Function) — testing + recommendations
Order lipid panels and give recommendations, at a premium price. Useful if you want testing bundled in, but you can also just [track the labs you already get](/blog/insidetracker-alternative/).

### Spreadsheets — free, but manual
Some people track lipids in a spreadsheet. It works, but there's no automatic extraction, no ratio calculation, and no optimal-range flagging.
