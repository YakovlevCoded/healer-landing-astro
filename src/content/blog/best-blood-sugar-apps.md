Blood sugar is one of the most important things to track for long-term health — about 96 million US adults have prediabetes and most don't know it ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes/php/data-research/index.html)). But "tracking blood sugar" means different things: daily fingersticks, a continuous sensor, or the lab markers (HbA1c, fasting glucose, fasting insulin) that show the bigger trend. The right app depends on which you're doing.

## The three kinds of blood-sugar apps

1. **Lab-trackers** — read your blood test results (HbA1c, glucose, insulin) and trend them over time.
2. **Glucose loggers** — record daily fingerstick readings, mainly for people with diabetes.
3. **CGM apps** — pair with a continuous glucose monitor for real-time data.

Most people watching metabolic health want the first — the lab-level trend — and that's where results most often get lost in scattered PDFs.

Ties results to symptoms and exports a doctor-ready PDF. Free to start, any lab.

### mySugr — best for daily glucose logging
A well-designed logbook for people with diabetes doing daily fingersticks — bolus calculator, reminders, reports. Focused on daily management rather than lab-marker trends.

### CGM apps (e.g. sensor companion apps) — best for real-time data
If you wear a continuous glucose monitor, these show minute-by-minute glucose and meal responses. Powerful, but sensor-dependent and more than many people need.

### General health apps — limited for blood sugar
Apple Health and similar can store a glucose value but don't calculate derived markers or flag early insulin resistance. (More on that in [Apple Health vs a dedicated lab tracker](/blog/apple-health-vs-lab-tracker/).)
