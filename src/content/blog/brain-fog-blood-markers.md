Brain fog is a frustrating complaint because it's real but hard to point at. You can't concentrate, words don't come, tasks that were easy feel like wading through mud — but there's no obvious injury or illness to blame. And because it's vague, it often gets waved off as stress or "just being tired."

Here's the more useful framing: **brain fog is a symptom, not a diagnosis.** And a surprising amount of it traces back to a handful of common, testable things in your blood.

## The stat most people don't know

[Vitamin B12 insufficiency](/blog/vitamin-b12-deficiency/) — a classic cause of brain fog and poor concentration — affects roughly **1 in 8 adults over 60** (about 12%), per [NIH Office of Dietary Supplements](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB12-HealthProfessional/) data, and it's more common in vegetarians, vegans, and people on long-term acid-reducing or metformin medication. Yet B12 is rarely tested unless someone asks. The same goes for iron: low ferritin causes mental fatigue well before it shows up as anemia, and iron deficiency is the [most common nutritional deficiency worldwide](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/anaemia) — yet ferritin is one of the most under-ordered markers there is.

## The markers worth checking for brain fog

- **Vitamin B12 and folate** — needed for healthy nerves and red blood cells; deficiency is a textbook cause of fog and difficulty concentrating.
- **Ferritin (iron stores)** — low iron impairs oxygen delivery and neurotransmitter production. Fog often comes with fatigue here. ([What low ferritin actually means](/blog/low-ferritin-guide/).)
- **Thyroid (TSH ± free T4)** — an underactive thyroid slows cognition, mood, and energy together. ([High TSH, explained](/blog/high-tsh-thyroid-explained/).)
- **Vitamin D** — widespread deficiency links to low mood and cognitive sluggishness.
- **Blood sugar (HbA1c + fasting insulin)** — glucose swings and [early insulin resistance](/blog/how-to-know-if-you-have-insulin-resistance/) cause the mid-afternoon mental crash.
- **Inflammation (CRP, homocysteine)** — chronic low-grade inflammation is increasingly linked to "cloudy" thinking.

If several of these overlap, the fog usually has a real, addressable driver.

## The "normal" trap — again

The reason people stay foggy for months is the same trap that hides fatigue: labs flag values only outside a very wide range. A B12 sitting at the bottom of normal, a TSH creeping toward the top, a ferritin of 18 — each can be reported "fine" while still clouding your thinking. What matters is **where you land in the optimal range and which way you're trending**, not whether you've crossed a red line. (This is the same pattern behind [persistent fatigue](/blog/always-tired-blood-markers/).)

4. **Talk to a clinician** before supplementing — the right move depends on the whole picture.
