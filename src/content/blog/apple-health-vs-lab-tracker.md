Apple Health is genuinely good at what it was built for: pulling together steps, heart rate, sleep, and workouts from your iPhone and Apple Watch into one continuous stream. If you want to know how you slept last night or whether your resting heart rate is drifting, it's right there.

But ask it a different question — *"is my ferritin trending down?"* or *"what does my HOMA-IR say about my blood sugar?"* — and it goes quiet. Blood work is a different kind of data, and it needs a different kind of tool.

## The stat most people don't know

The average adult accumulates **years of lab results scattered across patient portals, PDFs, and emails** — and studies of personal health records consistently find that most people can't produce their own past bloodwork when asked. The data exists; it's just fragmented and unreadable. A pile of numbers you can't see over time isn't much more useful than no data at all.

## What Apple Health does well

- **Continuous wearable data** — steps, heart rate, HRV, sleep, workouts
- **A single timeline** for metrics your devices capture automatically
- **Health Records** — if your provider is connected, it can pull in some clinical data
- **Privacy** — data stays largely on-device

For the everyday, sensor-driven side of health, it's hard to beat.

## Where it falls short for lab results

- **It doesn't read lab PDFs.** Most people get results as a PDF or a portal screenshot. Apple Health can't ingest those — you'd be typing values by hand.
- **It doesn't calculate derived markers.** Scores like HOMA-IR ([insulin resistance](/blog/how-to-know-if-you-have-insulin-resistance/)) or triglyceride-to-HDL ratio require combining values. Apple Health just stores the raw ones.
- **It doesn't flag optimal vs "normal."** A ferritin of 20 shows as a number, not as "functionally low." (More on that in [what low ferritin actually means](/blog/low-ferritin-guide/).)
- **It doesn't connect markers or symptoms.** It won't tell you that your rising [HbA1c](/blog/hba1c-explained/) sits alongside a high HOMA-IR, or that your [fatigue lines up with dropping iron](/blog/always-tired-blood-markers/).

None of this is a knock on Apple Health — it just wasn't designed to be a lab interpreter.

## The honest take

Use Apple Health for what it's great at — the continuous, wearable side of your health. For blood work specifically — uploading results, seeing trends across every test, calculating the markers that matter, and tying labs to how you actually feel — you want a tool built for it. They're not competitors so much as two halves of the picture.
