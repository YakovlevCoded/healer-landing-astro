Of all the numbers on a lipid panel, triglycerides are the one most tied to what you eat and how your metabolism is running. Cholesterol gets the attention, but a high triglyceride level is often the earliest and most responsive signal of a metabolic problem brewing — and it's frequently glossed over.

## What triglycerides are

Triglycerides are the main form of fat in your blood and your body's main way of storing and transporting energy. After a meal — especially one high in carbs, sugar, or alcohol — triglycerides rise as that energy is packaged up. Chronically high levels mean the system that clears them isn't keeping up, which is often an insulin problem.

## What the numbers mean

- **Below 150 mg/dL** — normal
- **150–199 mg/dL** — borderline high
- **200–499 mg/dL** — high
- **500+ mg/dL** — very high (and a pancreatitis risk at the top end)

They're measured **fasting**, because they spike after eating ([American Heart Association](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/cholesterol/hdl-good-ldl-bad-cholesterol-and-triglycerides)). A level over 150 is also one of the five criteria for [metabolic syndrome](/blog/metabolic-syndrome-markers/).

## The stat most people don't know

High triglycerides rarely travel alone. Paired with a low HDL, they form one of the clearest lipid signatures of [insulin resistance](/blog/how-to-know-if-you-have-insulin-resistance/) — so much so that the [triglyceride-to-HDL ratio](/blog/triglyceride-hdl-ratio/) is used as a surrogate marker for it ([Kim-Dorner et al., NCBI](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8110426/)). In other words, an elevated triglyceride number is often waving a flag about your blood sugar and insulin, not just your heart.

## Why they rise

The main drivers are highly modifiable:

- **Refined carbs and added sugar** — the biggest dietary lever; the liver converts excess into triglycerides
- **Alcohol** — raises triglycerides significantly, even in moderate amounts for some people
- **Insulin resistance** — impairs triglyceride clearance ([fasting insulin](/blog/fasting-insulin-test-guide/) is worth checking)
- **Excess weight and inactivity**
- Poorly controlled diabetes and certain medications

The upside of all that: because triglycerides respond so quickly to diet and insulin sensitivity, they often **drop fast** with the right changes — making them a satisfying marker to track.

## What to do

1. **Get a fasting triglyceride level** — non-fasting readings run higher.
2. **Read it with HDL and insulin** — the ratio reveals the metabolic angle.
3. **Target the drivers** — refined carbs, sugar, and alcohol are the fastest levers.
4. **Re-test after changes** — triglycerides move quickly, so they show progress fast. **Talk to a clinician**, especially if very high.
