You're productive in the morning, and then around 2 or 3pm it hits: focus dissolves, your eyelids get heavy, and suddenly a cookie feels like a medical necessity. The afternoon energy crash is so common it's treated as normal — but it's often a readable signal of what your blood sugar is doing across the day.

## The blood-sugar roller coaster

The classic crash is a swing. A lunch heavy in refined carbs spikes your glucose; your body releases insulin to bring it back down; and if that insulin response is exaggerated — as it is in [early insulin resistance](/blog/how-to-know-if-you-have-insulin-resistance/) — glucose overshoots downward into a relative low. Your brain reads the dip as an energy emergency and demands fast fuel: fatigue, fog, and a [sugar craving](/blog/sugar-cravings-insulin/), all at once.

## The stat most people don't know

About **96 million US adults — more than 1 in 3 — have prediabetes, and over 80% don't know it** ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes/php/data-research/index.html)). One reason it hides: standard screening measures fasting glucose, which stays normal for years, rather than [fasting insulin](/blog/fasting-insulin-test-guide/), which rises early ([StatPearls / NCBI](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK507839/)). So the exact mechanism that exaggerates afternoon crashes is often present and undetected.

## What to check

- **Fasting insulin + glucose (HOMA-IR)** — reveals early insulin resistance behind the swings, before glucose looks abnormal.
- **HbA1c** — your 3-month blood-sugar average ([what it means](/blog/hba1c-explained/)).
- **Ferritin and thyroid** — if the tiredness is constant rather than just post-lunch, low iron or an [underactive thyroid](/blog/high-tsh-thyroid-explained/) may be adding to it (see [always tired](/blog/always-tired-blood-markers/)).

## It's not always just blood sugar

Afternoon slumps also ride on **sleep debt, dehydration, big high-carb lunches, and caffeine timing** (a coffee that wears off mid-afternoon). Those are worth addressing first. But when crashes are frequent, severe, and paired with cravings and midsection weight, the metabolic angle is worth testing rather than guessing.

## What to do

1. **Notice the pattern** — crashes plus cravings plus midsection weight point toward the blood-sugar swing.
2. **Check insulin, not just glucose** — ask for fasting insulin so HOMA-IR can be calculated.
3. **Fix the easy inputs first** — sleep, hydration, a lighter/protein-forward lunch, caffeine timing.
4. **Track over time** and **talk to a clinician** about steadying blood sugar.
