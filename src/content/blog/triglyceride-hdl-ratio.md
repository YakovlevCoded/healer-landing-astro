There's a useful number hiding in a test you've almost certainly already had — and almost no one calculates it. Your standard lipid panel already contains everything you need for one of the best cheap signals of **insulin resistance**: the triglyceride-to-HDL ratio. It costs nothing extra, and it often reveals metabolic risk that total cholesterol completely misses.

## How to calculate it

Take two numbers straight off your [lipid panel](/blog/cholesterol-numbers-explained/):

> **Triglyceride-to-HDL ratio = triglycerides ÷ HDL** (same units — mg/dL in the US)

Example: triglycerides 150, HDL 50 → ratio **3.0**. That's it — no new test, no fasting beyond what the lipid panel already needs.

## The stat most people don't know

The triglyceride-to-HDL ratio is **one of the best surrogate markers of insulin resistance** you can get from a routine lipid panel — outperforming triglycerides or HDL alone in several studies ([Kim-Dorner et al., NCBI](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8110426/)). The reason it works: [insulin resistance](/blog/how-to-know-if-you-have-insulin-resistance/) tends to push triglycerides **up** and HDL **down** at the same time, so dividing one by the other amplifies that exact signal.

## What your ratio means

In US units (mg/dL):

- **Below ~2** — generally favorable
- **~2–3 and rising** — increasingly associated with insulin resistance
- **Above ~3** — a stronger flag for metabolic risk

Study cutoffs range from roughly **1.5 to 3** depending on the population and ethnicity, so treat it as a signal to investigate rather than a hard diagnosis. Crucially, the ratio can be elevated **even when your total cholesterol is "normal"** — which is exactly why it catches what a single number hides.

## Why it beats total cholesterol for metabolic risk

Total cholesterol lumps everything together and can look reassuring while masking a high-triglyceride, low-HDL pattern — the classic metabolic-risk signature. The triglyceride-to-HDL ratio isolates that pattern, tying your lipid panel directly to your [blood-sugar and insulin picture](/blog/fasting-insulin-test-guide/) and the broader [metabolic syndrome](/blog/metabolic-syndrome-markers/) cluster.

## What to do

1. **Calculate it from your last lipid panel** — triglycerides ÷ HDL.
2. **Read it in context** — a raised ratio suggests checking fasting insulin and glucose too.
3. **Track it over time** — improvement in insulin sensitivity usually lowers the ratio.
4. **Talk to a clinician** — use it as one input into your overall cardiometabolic risk.
